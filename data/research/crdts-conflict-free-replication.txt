Conflict-free replicated data types are data structures designed so that replicas can be updated independently and merged without coordination while always converging. Grow-only sets, last-writer-wins registers, and counters that only increment are examples. Merge operations are commutative, associative, and idempotent, typically built on join semilattices. CRDTs power offline-first apps and collaborative editors, trading strong consistency for availability under partition, as formalized by the CAP theorem.
