Byzantine fault tolerance allows a distributed system to reach agreement even when some nodes behave arbitrarily, crashing, lying, or colliding. The PBFT protocol tolerates up to f faulty replicas among 3f+1 total by exchanging pre-prepare, prepare, and commit messages in three phases. The classic result is that consensus requires 3f+1 nodes to survive f Byzantine faults. PBFT is used in permissioned blockchains and mission-critical replication where silent data corruption must not break correctness.
